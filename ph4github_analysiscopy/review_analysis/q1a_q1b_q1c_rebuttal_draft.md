## Local-evidence summary

- The implemented pipeline is: Bayesian titration controller -> imitation learning from Bayesian-generated trajectories -> RL fine-tuning of the distilled policy.
- The imitation dataset is not a generic "simulation-only" dataset; it is generated by repeatedly calling the Bayesian teacher policy (`review_outputs/logs/main_code3_cell_3.py`, especially the `select_best_action` calls around lines 582 and 597 and the posterior update routine at line 520).
- The imitation network in the local code is a `5 -> 256 -> 256 -> 1000` classifier trained with cross-entropy, not the older 64-unit description (`review_outputs/logs/main_code3_cell_5.py`, lines 15-17 and 146-148).
- The RL stage in the local code is REINFORCE with 500 episodes by default, not PPO (`review_outputs/logs/main_code3_cell_9.py`, lines 372-374 and 470).
- The raw simulation logs support success rates of 90.30% (Bayesian), 93.87% (imitation), and 94.30% (reinforcement) (`data/bayesian.txt`, `data/m_network.txt`, `data/reinforced_network.txt`; see also `reviewer_analysis_report.md`).
- The Bayesian controller does expose posterior means and standard deviations and uses uncertainty in action selection (`review_outputs/logs/main_code3_cell_3.py`, lines 224-228, 430-479, and 520-544).
- However, the current local interpretability traces do not support a strong claim that approximately eight titration steps are sufficient to identify the true number of species or their true pKa values. The safer claim is that the Bayesian module provides structured intermediate latent estimates and uncertainty-aware control.
- No distinct baseline was found for "train a neural network directly on simulated data without a Bayesian teacher." The current imitation model is a Bayesian-teacher distillation model, not a pure direct-supervised baseline.

## Recommended positioning for Reviewer 1a

Use a narrower novelty claim. Do not claim novelty merely because RL or Bayesian ideas are applied to pH control. The defendable distinction is the staged integration:

1. a chemistry-grounded Bayesian controller that performs online posterior updating during titration,
2. distillation of that controller into a fast neural policy,
3. optional RL refinement of the distilled policy,
4. validation across simulation and multiple real titration scenarios.

Suggested manuscript-side wording:

> Rather than claiming the first use of Bayesian or RL ideas in pH control, we position this work as a hybrid decision pipeline that combines online chemistry-informed Bayesian inference, policy distillation, and RL refinement within one titration framework, and then validates the resulting controllers on heterogeneous real samples.

## Recommended positioning for Reviewer 1b

Use a weaker interpretability claim. The current evidence supports:

- explicit posterior means / standard deviations,
- uncertainty-aware action moderation,
- a structured chemical prior in the decision loop.

The current evidence does not robustly support:

- definitive identification of the number of species,
- definitive recovery of true pKa values,
- a claim that approximately eight experiments are generally sufficient for such identification.

Suggested manuscript-side wording:

> In practice, the Bayesian layer improves interpretability by exposing intermediate posterior quantities, such as inferred effective acidity parameters and their uncertainty, which directly influence the recommended dosing volume. We therefore use "interpretability" here in the sense of a visible, chemistry-structured internal state, rather than a guaranteed identification of the exact underlying species composition from a small number of titration steps.

## Recommended positioning for Reviewer 1c

Be explicit that a pure direct-supervised baseline is not yet present in the local code. The strongest defendable response is conceptual plus implementation-grounded:

- the Bayesian stage injects chemical structure into label generation,
- it provides uncertainty-aware conservative behavior before RL,
- it reduces the burden on the neural policy to discover sensible dosing logic from scratch,
- it yields a controller that can still be inspected mechanistically.

Do not claim an empirical advantage over a direct simulated-data network unless such a baseline is actually run.

Suggested manuscript-side wording:

> At present, our local implementation compares Bayesian control, Bayesian-teacher imitation learning, and RL fine-tuning, but it does not yet include a separate neural network trained directly on simulator-generated labels without the Bayesian teacher. We therefore revised the text to frame the advantage of the Bayesian stage as mechanistic guidance and structured label generation, rather than as an already established superiority over every direct-supervised alternative.

## Draft rebuttal text

### Reviewer 1a

We thank the reviewer for raising this important point. We agree that the manuscript should not imply that Bayesian or RL ideas themselves are new to pH control. We have therefore revised the positioning of the work to make the distinction more precise. In our local implementation, the contribution is the integration of three stages within one titration framework: (i) an online Bayesian controller that updates latent acid-base parameters during titration using charge-balance and equilibrium calculations, (ii) an imitation-learning stage that distills this chemistry-informed controller into a fast neural policy, and (iii) a reinforcement-learning stage that further refines the distilled policy. In addition, the framework is evaluated not only in simulation but also across multiple real titration scenarios. We have revised the manuscript to emphasize this hybrid pipeline and to avoid any broader claim that RL- or Bayesian-based pH control itself is unprecedented.

### Reviewer 1b

We appreciate this comment and agree that our original wording on interpretability was too strong. Based on the local code and analysis, the practical interpretability provided by the Bayesian stage is that it maintains explicit posterior quantities, including inferred acidity-related parameters and their posterior uncertainty, and these quantities directly affect the next dosing decision. In particular, higher posterior uncertainty leads the controller to act more conservatively. However, we do not believe the current evidence is sufficient to claim that approximately eight titration steps can, in general, identify the true number of species present in a buffer or recover their true pKa values. We have therefore narrowed the manuscript language accordingly and now describe the Bayesian layer as providing a chemistry-structured, uncertainty-aware internal representation rather than definitive chemical-species identification from a small number of measurements.

### Reviewer 1c

We thank the reviewer for this helpful question. In the current local implementation, the imitation-learning model is trained on trajectories generated by the Bayesian controller, so it should be regarded as Bayesian-teacher distillation rather than a neural network trained directly on simulated data without Bayesian guidance. We did not find a separate direct-supervised baseline of that type in the present codebase, and we have revised the manuscript to state this clearly. At the conceptual level, the role of the Bayesian policy is to inject mechanistic chemical structure into the training signal, expose uncertainty during decision-making, and provide a conservative, chemically informed teacher policy before RL refinement. We therefore now present these points as the motivation for the Bayesian stage, while avoiding any unsupported claim that it has already been shown to outperform every direct-simulation neural baseline in practice.
